# Day 4 and Day 5 Character Ideas

This is a planning guide, not a scene-by-scene script. It uses the character summary, the murder-plan flowchart, the existing investigation rules, and the tone already established in the game.

## What Days 4 and 5 need to accomplish

The investigation routes currently give their decisive suspect-elimination clues on visits **1, 3, and 6**. Days 4 and 5 should therefore create progress without crossing anyone out yet.

- **Day 4:** complicate what the player thinks they know. Give the character a problem their usual persona cannot easily solve.
- **Day 5:** create the route's most personal moment so far, then point directly toward the category that will matter on Day 6.
- Let each scene contain a **soft lead**: an observation worth remembering, but too uncertain to eliminate a suspect.
- Give every day one meaningful choice about the work and one about the relationship. Avoid choices that are merely "nice / neutral / rude."
- Make the character's power create a practical or emotional problem, not just provide a shortcut.
- End Day 5 with a concrete reason to return for Day 6: a delayed result, a missing person or object, a scheduled final test, or a newly noticed contradiction.
- Do not let a soft lead reveal the selected killer or duplicate a hard clue already awarded on Day 1 or Day 3.

## Razzle Dazzle

Razzle should remain cheerful, chaotic, social, and genuinely competent when real superhero work matters. Her constant fire is funniest and most affecting when it gets in the way of ordinary life.

### Day 4 — The silhouette problem

**Core idea:** A witness is confident about seeing the killer's shape but keeps mixing up **height, build, posture, and distance**. Razzle proposes an energetic alleyway reconstruction using stand-ins and her fire as a movable light source.

What to do:

- Let Razzle initially treat the reconstruction like a game show or wrestling entrance. It keeps the scene funny and fits her energy.
- Make her flames distort the shadows or scare the witness, forcing her to accept that more enthusiasm is not always helpful.
- Give the player a practical choice: adjust the light, change the stand-ins' posture, or calm and question the witness. Each option can reveal a different piece of flavor, but none should eliminate a suspect.
- Use the flowchart's "hints about build and height" idea. The useful conclusion is that one of those qualities may have been misread because the figure was hunched, carrying something, or standing on uneven ground.
- Give Razzle a competent moment when she notices a human detail everyone else overlooked—how the witness describes movement, clothing, or the way the person occupied space.

Relationship beat:

- Razzle admits that people make instant assumptions about her too: they see the flames before they see her. The player can relate that to the witness's unreliable silhouette without turning the scene into a speech.
- A high-affection player can help regulate the flame by standing close and giving her calm, specific directions. The intimacy comes from trust around a power that can hurt people.

End beat:

- The witness remembers that the lighting changed when the killer passed beneath a sign. That suggests there may be one visual trait—eventually hair color—that can be recovered more reliably in a later interview.

### Day 5 — The quiet follow-up

**Core idea:** Razzle expects another loud field day, but the only available lead is a frightened witness who will talk only somewhere calm. The scene forces her to slow down and listen.

What to do:

- Put the meeting on the ATLAS roof, a fireproof loading area, or another place where Razzle does not have to apologize for existing.
- Let the witness recall flashes of hair under changing light but not enough to name a dependable color yet. This points toward Day 6 without awarding the hair clue early.
- Have Razzle catch herself before suggesting answers. This shows growth from enthusiastic interviewer to responsible investigator.
- Offer a choice between pressing for details, taking a break, or asking the witness to describe the scene through another sense. The best investigation choice should be patience, not simply selecting the obvious clue.

Relationship beat:

- After the witness leaves, give Razzle a smaller, sincere scene about wanting to be valued as a person and not only as "the fire girl" or comic relief.
- The player can reassure her, flirt without reducing her to the fire gimmick, or encourage her growing competence.

End beat:

- The witness remembers a second person or object that could verify how the light altered the killer's hair. Razzle schedules the final follow-up for Day 6.

Continuity note (Resolved):

- Day 3's hard clue is canonically **height** (Brandon's doorway observation eliminates Short, Average, or Tall). Day 4's window reenactment and Day 5's tape analysis explicitly establish that "build" was an unreliable optical illusion caused by the killer hunching forward while clutching a bulky package, while the sloped lawn distorted perceived height. Day 5 also embeds a subtle soft clue for hand injuries (the killer flinching when touching brick) and confirms loose hair strands under the headlights for Day 6's reconstruction.

## Madeline

Madeline should be serious, strategic, cautious, passionate once engaged, and more socially awkward than she wants to admit. Her strongest material comes from her need to prepare for every failure—including the possibility that her own mind or methods cannot be trusted.

### Day 4 — The wrong machine

**Core idea:** Madeline builds a prototype intended to separate elemental residue from physical wound trauma. It gives an impossible result because her model assumes each wound has only one cause.

What to do:

- The prototype reports Fire, Ice, and Light in the same tiny area. The machine is functioning; the input model is wrong.
- Let the player question the assumption, offer to stay for a complete retest, or insult Madeline for missing it.
- Separating the events by sequence reveals how elemental damage, displaced surroundings, and direct physical contact combined during the struggle.
- The corrected wound pattern subtly suggests `Bruised Knuckles`, `Scuffed Hands`, or `None`. Never name the category or add it to the notebook.
- Make the failure useful because it improves the final Day 6 comparison, not because failure magically solves the case.

Relationship beat:

- Madeline is comfortable being seen as brilliant but uncomfortable being seen while wrong. The player earns trust by treating uncertainty as part of science rather than mocking or blindly flattering her.
- A flirt option can compare her intensity to the overheated equipment, but the stronger romantic choice is offering to stay for the tedious retest.

End beat:

- Madeline realizes her helmet and scanner need a live neural-feedback calibration. She asks the player to control the safety cutoff during the more personal Day 5 test.

### Day 5 — The consent test

**Core idea:** Madeline's calibration method briefly changes stress, attention, or emotional expression. Because she is deeply concerned about mental control, she refuses to use it until the player helps design strict consent and shutdown rules.

What to do:

- Keep the effect mild and reversible—closer to a suit feedback loop than actual mind control.
- Use all three agreed safeguards: a manual cutoff controlled by the player, a verbal check-in sequence, and a strict time limit. Winston is not present; Madeline's trust is expressed by giving the player control.
- During calibration, Madeline's normal guarded presentation slips. She may become extremely candid, visibly nervous, or unable to hide how much she enjoys working with the player.
- The helmet calibrates scanner noise but does not reveal the killer's personality or evidence category. Afterward, a separate pass over the preserved sample subtly shows careful cleaning or uncontrolled dirt and transfer, hinting at `Clean` or `Messy` organization without logging it.

Relationship beat:

- Reduced emotional filtering makes Madeline accidentally admit how much she enjoys or respects the player's presence.
- Reward the player for respecting the first stop condition even if pushing longer would produce "better" data. Continuing is the route's most serious relationship violation.

End beat:

- The calibration succeeds. Madeline can now remove scanner noise during the final Fire/Ice/Light comparison on Day 6.

## Winston

Winston should be practical, sarcastic, blunt, protective, and smarter than his lazy presentation suggests. His power nullification makes him both the team's safety net and the person who knows how violating it feels to have a power taken away.

### Day 4 — Power tells

**Core idea:** Winston conducts a deliberately casual witness follow-up in his
cluttered office. Darts, takeout, and his fool act make the witness stop
performing for a formal interrogation. Their description quietly reveals the
killer's clean-versus-messy habits, with a secondary hand-condition detail when
needed to keep the final deduction logically possible.

What to do:

- Let the player join Winston's casual act, observe quietly, or request a formal approach.
- Relate the observation to broad setting tendencies: Fire users often run passionate, Ice users often present as cold or restrained, and Light users tend toward cleanliness and order. Treat them as tendencies, never identity rules.
- A minor uncontrolled power moment lets Winston demonstrate precise single-target dampening. He releases the witness as soon as they settle and never cancels the player's intuition.
- Keep both observations out of the formal notebook. Winston notices the quiet physical tell but does not announce its meaning.

Relationship beat:

- Takeout and darts lead naturally into Winston's history with Ulysses. Winston conceived ATLAS; thirteen-year-old Ulysses supplied the planning, forms, and unnervingly adult competence, while Winston's established superhero reputation made people take the organization seriously.
- Winston is proud of the partnership and comfortable sharing credit. He never directly states that Ulysses understands him best.

### Day 5 — The minute after

**Core idea:** A frightened witness with heightened hearing returns to ATLAS.
Winston constructs the room from office furniture, masking tape, a coat rack,
and a pizza box so the witness can rebuild the minute after Enrico died without
returning to the crime scene.

What to do:

- The player chooses a reconstruction role and controls the witness's pace.
- Gentle pacing and grounding humor both work. Forcing the witness onward is a serious relationship loss, and Winston immediately intervenes.
- Winston plays the killer while the witness confirms sounds and movement. The reconstruction suggests Panicked, Calculated, or No Visible Reaction but remains unlogged because two moments are not yet securely ordered.
- Day 6 uses a second written statement and verified telephone timeline to order those moments and formally retain the matching reaction trio.

Relationship beat:

- While dismantling the fake scene, Winston explains that people reveal more around an apparent fool—but also admits that he genuinely enjoys being ridiculous.
- High affection allows suggestive hand contact without a confession. The unmistakable waist touch remains the Day 6 payoff.

## Dhampir

Dhampir should remain extremely chill, confident, strange, and casual even when discussing violence. The tension in his route comes from the fact that his methods can be frightening while his intentions are sincere.

### Day 4 — What happened afterward

**Core idea:** Dhampir revisits the crime scene in casual clothes and reconstructs only the minute after Enrico died. The killer's movement, cleanup, and departure provide a subtle indication of their reaction without adding a formal notebook clue.

What to do:

- Describe the room differently for the saved killer: hurried disorder, deliberate cleanup, or an unchanged walk out. Never name the `Panicked`, `Calculated`, or `None` label in dialogue or record it in the notebook.
- Let the player question Dhampir's casual attitude toward death. He explains that it is sincerely his worldview rather than a coping mechanism.
- Hint at the Monster Hunters as the first family that accepted him and at the efficient revenge he took after their deaths, without recounting his entire history.
- Keep the visit focused on observation and dialogue; there is no threat or minigame.

Investigation purpose:

- Provide the first unlogged hint that distinguishes the final three suspects: how the killer behaved immediately after Enrico died.
- Establish that the attack and the killer's reaction are separate questions.

Relationship beat:

- Dhampir explains that being casual is simply how he views death, not proof that he disrespects the victim.
- The player can ask for more care, accept the coping mechanism, or meet him with dark humor.

End beat:

- Dhampir notices the scene's inventory does not quite match the reconstruction. Something small may have been torn from or left by the attacker.

### Day 5 — Wounds versus build

**Core idea:** Dhampir and the player compare the wound report with a controlled sparring demonstration or mannequin reconstruction. The point is to learn what the evidence cannot prove.

What to do:

- Let Dhampir demonstrate how the same wound can come from different builds, weapons, powers, or fighting styles.
- Describe force, angle, and reach subtly enough for the player to infer the killer's build without adding it to the notebook.
- Use his phasing or flight to show how supernatural movement breaks ordinary forensic assumptions.
- Avoid introducing a new hard injury clue; Day 3 already owns that category.

Relationship beat:

- A sparring setup allows playful tension while demonstrating his patience, precision, phasing, and intolerance for reckless action.
- He jokes about the Monster Hunters' deaths before sincerely explaining that he kills efficiently because prolonging a fight gets innocent people killed.

End beat:

- The reconstruction isolates where a tiny torn item would have landed. Dhampir arranges a focused Day 6 search for the missing hair, tooth, or ear fragment category. Day 6 positively retains the matching three-person category.

## Nicky

Nicky is separate from the attached summary's Ika/Nikki/Niki entry, which corresponds to Ica's gravity power. For Nicky, use the game's established characterization: an LAPD officer, serious and by-the-book while working, fun off duty, super-strong, sensitive to pheromones, and often forced to keep ATLAS legally grounded.

### Day 4 — Reports versus reality

**Core idea:** Nicky discovers that the police report, booking photos, and ATLAS's physical reconstruction do not agree about the suspect's build or injuries.

What to do:

- Turn the discrepancy into a records-board activity: match statements, timestamps, photos, and medical notes, then identify which detail came from assumption rather than observation.
- Let Nicky's ant strength become relevant when she casually recreates an action that would require a large build from an ordinary person. This proves that build alone cannot establish force in a powered setting.
- Use her pheromone sensitivity carefully: she can notice fear or stress in a room, but it should not function as a lie detector.
- Put her between police procedure and an ATLAS shortcut, especially if Dhampir's earlier handling of evidence was questionable.

Relationship beat:

- Nicky admits that both groups expect her to clean up their mistakes while neither fully treats her as one of their own.
- The player can back her procedure, ask where procedure failed, or help find a lawful workaround. The best choice should respect why the rule exists rather than merely obey authority.

End beat:

- One disputed item cannot be resolved from paperwork. A preserved sample must be checked in person on Day 5.

### Day 5 — The simple blood test

**Core idea:** Nicky escorts a sample through a chain-of-custody problem. The intended test is simple, but a mislabeled seal, missing signature, or suspiciously tidy correction makes the result unsafe to use as evidence.

What to do:

- Let the player inspect the seal, log, storage time, and handwriting before choosing whether to test, quarantine, or report the problem.
- A quick presumptive test may confirm that the material is blood or that the sample is still viable. It should not repeat Madeline's Day 3 blood-type elimination.
- Make the real discovery procedural and scientific: the intact inner vial provides a limited A/B/O and Rh reaction, while the unsigned outer correction prevents the result from becoming a formal elimination. This becomes a subtle player-facing hint for the final trio.
- If the player pushes for an immediate result, let Nicky explain that evidence obtained carelessly can collapse a correct case.

Relationship beat:

- After the official work, give them a short off-duty decompression scene with a beer, vending-machine snack, or episode of a show. Nicky finally lets the player see the easygoing person underneath the badge.
- The player can help her stop working for ten minutes, ask why she chose police work, or flirt by offering to be the one person she does not have to supervise.

End beat:

- The sample is secured and its limited blood-profile reaction is preserved as an investigative lead. Newly returned warrants, interview images, and a behavioral profile provide the concrete reason to return for the Day 6 personal-hygiene comparison.

## Ica

Ica is playful, confident, creative, and comfortable experimenting with mass and gravity. Her route is the deliberately low-investigation hangout route, but Days 4 and 5 should reveal that "lazy" is partly a pose and set up her serious Day 6 encounter.

### Day 4 — Eating competition

**Core idea:** Keep the flowchart's three approaches—**cheat, flirt, or play fair**—but make each a distinct expression of how the player relates to Ica.

What to do:

- **Cheat:** Ask Ica to alter the food's weight, slide plates with gravity, or hide portions. The comedy should come from mass changing weight but not volume, so the clever shortcut creates a new problem.
- **Flirt:** Distract Ica with escalating banter. This should succeed only if prior affection supports it; otherwise she sees through the tactic and turns it back on the player.
- **Play fair:** Use a short timing, rhythm, or endurance challenge. Ica respects the commitment even if the player loses.
- Allow Ica to cheat regardless of what the player chooses, but in a playful, discoverable way. The important choice is whether the player laughs, counters creatively, or becomes genuinely upset.

Character and relationship goal:

- Show that Ica loves games because rules give her something she can manipulate without permanently disappointing anyone.
- After the competition, let her briefly admit that people assume she never cares because she avoids obligations before she can fail at them.
- A strong response enjoys the joke but does not let her use humor to dodge the whole conversation.

End beat:

- Ica proposes a more ambitious game for tomorrow: pranking Ulysses, the one person who can theoretically see it coming.

### Day 5 — Prank Ulysses

**Core idea:** The challenge is not just pulling a prank; it is designing one that defeats future sight without causing harm or creating more work for the team.

What to do:

- Give the player three plans: a clever harmless gravity gag, a chaotic plan with collateral risk, or a randomized setup whose final trigger neither conspirator knows in advance.
- Let Ulysses's future sight make fixed plans fail comically. Randomness, player improvisation, or Ica changing an object's mass at the last instant creates the only genuine surprise.
- Include a point where the prank begins to endanger evidence, equipment, or a bystander. Ica immediately uses her power with precision to save the situation. This is the first clear crack in her "I do not care" persona.
- The best outcome is not humiliating Ulysses. It is getting a rare laugh or startled reaction from him without undermining his role.

Character and relationship goal:

- Ica admits she likes the player because they can play along without either worshipping her power or ordering her to grow up.
- The player can tell her that caring does not make her boring, challenge her to help when something truly matters, or turn the cleanup into a private flirt scene.

End beat:

- During cleanup, Ica notices a person repeatedly watching the building, an object moving against its expected weight, or Ulysses reacting to a future he cannot explain. She jokes about it, but remembers the detail. This naturally leads to spotting the killer and the planned game-of-chicken encounter on Day 6.

## Shared implementation guardrails

- Track affection outcomes separately from evidence outcomes. A player should be able to make a good deduction while upsetting the character, or make an emotionally supportive choice that does not improve the case.
- Use soft-lead wording such as **might**, **could**, **doesn't prove**, and **worth checking** on Days 4 and 5.
- Do not cross suspects out in the notebook on these days. If desired, add a separate "Unconfirmed Leads" line that never alters `remainingSuspects`.
- Keep each day to one main location change, one interactive problem, one personal reveal, and one end hook. That will keep six routes feasible for the jam.
- Reuse supporting characters to make the ensemble feel connected: Ulysses for rules and future-sight tension, Freddy for practical observations, Winston for power-safety backup, and Madeline/Nicky for lab or evidence handoffs.
- Day 5 should feel emotionally close even on a low-affection route. Low affection can mean professional respect, guarded honesty, or comic friction rather than a scene with no development.
